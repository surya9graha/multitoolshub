Introduction to Headline Capitalization and Title Case

Whether you are writing blog posts, drafting academic papers, or preparing magazine columns, formatting headlines correctly is vital. Different publishers require distinct rules for capitalization. Our online Title Case Converter automatically capitalizes your text based on major style guides: AP, Chicago, Wikipedia, or simple Capital Case.

Our tool runs entirely client-side, formatting input titles in your browser window.

Why Stylebook Guidelines Matter for Authors

Headline capitalization is not just about capitalizing every word. Major stylebooks (like AP Stylebook or Chicago Manual of Style) specify that minor words (such as articles, short prepositions, and coordinate conjunctions) should remain lowercased unless they start or end the title.

Real-Life Use Cases for Title Case Conversion

  • Blogging: Capitalizing blog article headlines for better visual appeal and click-through rates.
  • Academic Writing: Formatting essay and research paper titles to comply with Chicago Manual guidelines.
  • Content Curation: Standardizing title cases across multiple curated posts.

Comparing Major Title Capitalization Rules

Each style guide has unique rules for determining which words to lowercase:

  • AP Stylebook Rules: Lowercases articles (a, an, the), coordinate conjunctions (and, but, or, for), and prepositions of three or fewer letters (of, to, by, in, on, at, with).
  • Chicago Manual of Style: Lowercases all prepositions regardless of length (including from, into, onto, than, via, per).
  • Wikipedia Stylebook: Lowercases similar prepositions and articles, standardizing layouts for wiki entries.
  • Simple Capital Case: Capitalizes the first letter of every single word without exception.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Are the first and last words of a title always capitalized?

A: Yes. Under AP, Chicago, and Wikipedia guidelines, the first and last words of a title must be capitalized, even if they are minor prepositions or articles.

Q: Does this tool upload my texts?

A: No. All processing runs locally in your browser tab, ensuring privacy.

Q: What is the rule for hyphenated words?

A: Most stylebooks recommend capitalizing both parts of hyphenated words (e.g. `Step-by-Step`), which this tool handles.

Q: Can I use this for book titles?

A: Yes. Book titles are typically formatted using Chicago Manual rules, which this tool supports.
