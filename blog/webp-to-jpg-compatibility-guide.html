Solving Email Marketing Image Compatibility: WebP vs JPG

Updated: April 2026 9 Min Read By MultiTools Compatibility Experts

If you build modern websites, you have likely embraced the WebP image format. WebP offers incredible compression, rendering high-resolution photographs at a fraction of the file size of a traditional JPG. However, if you are a digital marketer carrying those same WebP assets over into your HTML email newsletters, you are making a catastrophic mistake.

The email rendering ecosystem is notoriously archaic. While modern web browsers all support WebP, millions of users read their email on legacy desktop clients (like older versions of Microsoft Outlook) or custom enterprise mail apps. In these environments, WebP graphics will completely fail to load, leaving your subscribers staring at ugly, broken image icons. To ensure your marketing campaigns execute flawlessly, you must revert to legacy formatting.

1. Converting WebP for Maximum Compatibility

When preparing assets for an email campaign, the golden rule is absolute maximum compatibility. You must assume your reader is using an outdated mail client on a corporate network. For complex photographs and hero banners, the JPG format remains the undisputed, universally supported king of the internet.

Before uploading your campaign assets to Mailchimp or HubSpot, you must pass them through a client-side WebP to JPG Converter. This utility uses your browser's HTML5 Canvas to instantly redraw the modern WebP pixels into a standard, bulletproof JPG file. The resulting file will be slightly larger in kilobytes, but it guarantees that every single subscriber will actually see your advertisement.

2. Handling Logos and Graphics

If the asset you are dealing with is a company logo or a graphical icon with text, you should not convert it to a JPG, as the lossy compression will create blurry artifacts around the lettering. Instead, these assets must be converted to PNG.

By using a JPG to PNG Converter (or WebP to PNG workflow), you preserve the sharp vector lines of your branding. Furthermore, PNG supports alpha-channel transparency, ensuring your logo blends perfectly if your subscriber reads their email in "Dark Mode."

3. Embedding Critical Icons via Base64

Email clients aggressively block external images by default to protect users from tracking pixels. This means your subscribers must manually click "Download Images" to see your graphics. If your email layout relies on tiny UI icons (like a magnifying glass or a social media logo) to be readable, the layout will look broken initially.

To bypass this image block, developers can embed critical icons directly into the email's HTML code using Base64. By passing a small icon through an Image to Base64 Encoder, you translate the visual graphic into a raw text string. When you paste this string into the HTML <img src=""> tag, the email client renders the icon instantly without requiring the user to download external assets. (Note: Only use this for very small icons, as Base64 strings significantly increase the total kilobyte size of the HTML file).

Common Email Marketing Mistakes

  • Uploading Massive Files: Even though you converted your WebP to a JPG, you must still compress it. Email clients often clip or completely reject messages if the total payload exceeds 102KB. Always compress your JPGs before sending.
  • Ignoring Alt Text: Because many email clients block images by default, your alt="Description" text will be the only thing the user sees. Never leave your alt tags blank; use them to write compelling copy that encourages the user to download the images.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why doesn't Outlook support modern web standards?

Many desktop versions of Microsoft Outlook use the Microsoft Word rendering engine to display HTML emails, rather than a modern web browser engine like Chromium. This restricts developers to coding practices from the early 2000s.

Is it safe to convert client assets using a browser tool?

Yes, but only if the tool is strictly client-side. When you use our converters, the image processing happens entirely within your local browser RAM. The proprietary marketing asset is never uploaded to an external server.

Can I use GIFs in email marketing?

Yes. The GIF format is nearly as universally supported as JPG. However, some newer versions of Outlook will only play the first frame of the animation and then freeze, so ensure the first frame contains the most important information.

Conclusion

Email marketing is an incredibly lucrative channel, but it requires strict adherence to legacy technical standards. By abandoning WebP in favor of converted JPGs and PNGs, and strategically embedding Base64 icons, you can guarantee a beautiful, unbroken experience for every single subscriber on your list.

2. WebP vs JPG: What changed?

WebP uses advanced predictive coding to store images. It basically calculates what the next block of pixels should look like based on the previous ones, allowing for tiny file sizes. JPG, while older, is the "Universal Language" of devices. While WebP is great for *serving* on the web, JPG is better for *sharing* across the digital ecosystem.

10. Common Migration Mistakes

The main mistake is assuming JPG will be smaller. It won't be. Since WebP is more advanced, a 50KB WebP will likely become a 150KB JPG. You are trading space for *compatibility*. Another error is trying to convert a **transparent WebP** to JPG; similar to PNG, the transparency will be replaced by a solid background.

Q: Can I convert an animated WebP to an animated JPG?
